02 · Process

Five steps, in the order the steel demands

01 / 05 — Forge

01

Forge

The bar goes into the coal forge near 2,050 °F and comes out longer, thinner, and honest. Ten to fourteen heats to draw a blade — the steel tells you when.

02

Grind

A 60-grit belt sets the bevel; a 400-grit finish decides how the patina will land. The grinder only removes — what remains is the knife.

03

Quench

Foil-wrapped, soaked at 1,475 °F, pulled into fast oil. The difference between a blade and a piece of metal is about four seconds.

04

Temper

Two hours at 375 °F, twice. Hardness you can trust at 59 HRC — hard enough to hold an edge, soft enough to forgive a drop.

05

Handle

Walnut scales drilled, pinned with brass, bedded in epoxy. Sanded to 400 grit, oiled, and the first edge put on by hand.

    Entry 01

    On Patina

    Stainless steel is a denial of time. Carbon steel is an agreement with it. A blade of 1084 leaves here bright, and from the first onion it meets, the steel begins to keep records — grays, blues, the bruised purple of a wet night in a leather sheath.

    “A patina is a ledger. Every meal, every rain, every pocket it rode in — written there.”

    We force the first page: a warm vinegar bath, an even gray, so the later writing has lines to sit on. After that the blade belongs to its kitchen. Some owners wax it back to shine each spring. Most stop, once they see where the work happens.

    Care is simple. Wash it. Dry it. A finger of oil before it goes in the drawer. Don’t grieve the marks — a knife that looks unused usually is.

    Entry 02

    Filed April 2025

    3 min read

    What the Grinder Takes

    Forging moves steel but never loses it; the bar that goes into the fire comes out the other side, rearranged. The grinder is different. It is the only honest aristocrat in the shop: it takes, and gives nothing back.

    “The grinder only removes. What’s left standing is the knife you meant.”

    There is a moment — scribe line lit blue, belt humming at two thousand feet a minute — when the blade stops being a sketch. Everything on the wrong side of the line goes into the dust collector, and no heat will bring it back.

    Restoration is the same promise in reverse. Rust is just steel that forgot itself; we grind back to the line the first smith intended, and stop there. Knowing where to stop is the whole trade.
