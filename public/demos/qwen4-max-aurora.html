01 The Phenomenon

Solar wind meets the thin blue edge of everything.

A hundred million kilometres away, the sun exhales. Eight minutes later the light arrives; two days later the particles do. They spiral down the magnetic field lines, funnel toward the poles, and strike the upper air — and the atmosphere answers in colour.

Every hue is a fingerprint: an atom relaxing, releasing one precise quantum of light. Green is oxygen at 557.7 nanometres, taking its time. Red is the same atom, higher up, slower still. What looks like a curtain is a ledger of collisions, written across a thousand kilometres of sky.

02 The Spectrum

Four atoms. Four confessions.

Colour in the aurora is not decoration — it is altitude, energy, and patience made visible.

Oxygen · Green

The signature line. Excited atomic oxygen at 100–150 km, decaying in three quarters of a second.
