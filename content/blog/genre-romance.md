In ["Genre — Science Fiction: Worldbuilding at Scale"](/blog/genre-science-fiction-worldbuilding-at-scale) I said scale is which refusals travel. A culture that costs in the kitchen has to keep costing two cities later, including during the love scene. Today we stay in the love scene and ask whether it is a scene or a book.

The cheap definition of romance is two people plus heat. That definition will ruin the book, because heat is what the model is for. Ask for romance and you will get smoldering, a misunderstanding that clears in weather, and a last-chapter yes that nobody paid for. Completeness of feeling is a brochure with a pulse. A brochure is not a contract.

Romance Writers of America defines the genre by two elements: a central love story, and an emotionally satisfying and optimistic ending. Subplots are allowed. The love story has to be the plot. Historian Steve Ammidown puts the same rule in the language readers actually use: HEA or HFN, the way a mystery reader waits for a reveal. If you generate a tragic parting and file it under romance, you have written a different genre and insulted a contract.

By the end you should be able to hire a barrier the way you hired a well, catch the moment the model refunds it for tenderness or clarity, and tell a paid ending from a last paragraph that found a ring.

## Heat Is Furniture

In ["Genre Calibration: Training AI for Fiction vs Non-Fiction"](/blog/genre-calibration-training-ai-for-fiction-vs-non-fiction) I said the model has priors for genre furniture. Ask for romance and the furniture arrives: rain, a ball, a workplace with one desk too few, banter that is contempt with better lighting. That is not romance. That is a brochure with a couple.

Pamela Regis, in *A Natural History of the Romance Novel*, names eight essential elements: society defined, the meeting, the barrier, the attraction, the declaration, the point of ritual death, the recognition, and the betrothal. Sarah Frantz, editing romance after a career in the scholarship, has said she hammers Barrier hardest, because two people falling in love without narrative tension is sweet and is not a romance narrative. Romance writers call the point of ritual death the dark moment. The names differ. The physics does not.

You do not need to dump eight beats into a prompt. You already know what happens when you paste a constitution into every chapter. The model smuggles. Two or three constraints the page actually touches. She cannot take the transfer. He cannot be in that house. The name is an insult even in the dark. Then you run the barrier against what arrives.

Pride and Prejudice is taught as if the lesson were wit. The lesson is the barrier. Class, entailment, a first proposal that is an insult, a letter that is information, Lydia's elopement as the point of ritual death. The second proposal is betrothal because the first one cost. Generate "enemies to lovers, banter, rain" and you will get the furniture. You will not get Lydia. Lydia is the book. Jane Eyre does the same with a living wife and a proposal that cannot be kept. Heat is present. Heat is not the physics. If you generate the proposal without the attic, you have written a different novel and called it passion.

## The Brochure with a Pulse

Here is the AI accent of fake romance, and it is more specific than "the model doesn't understand love."

The model is fluent in the *sound* of desire. JD Lasica, writing for Authors.AI in 2024, printed untrained samples romance writers had pulled from Claude: smoldering eyes dark as sin; alabaster skin; virile manhood; a womanly core. That is not a taste problem. It is a prior. The corpus of bad heat is larger than the corpus of paid barriers. Fluency goes to the larger pile.

Purple prior. Every touch sears. Every gaze smolders. You hired two people. You received a catalog of adjectives that have never been used as cost.

Barrier holiday. The conflict is named so that it can be escaped. They work for competing firms; they kiss in the supply closet; the firms do not notice. In ["Genre — Science Fiction: Soft SF"](/blog/genre-science-fiction-soft-sf) I called this the taboo holiday: the unspeakable is spoken because the scene is intimate. Romance's version is the job that was a barrier on page forty and a meet-cute by page sixty. You hired a cost. A scene that spends the cost and then refunds it has no cost.

Completeness emotion. You asked for interiority, so the model supplies a functioning inner life: I have never felt this, I am terrified, I am also safe, I want, I should not want. In ["Novel II — Deep Craft: Showing vs Telling"](/blog/novel-ii-deep-craft-showing-vs-telling) I said telling is the model explaining the feeling so the reader will not have to work. Romance is where that habit looks like craft. Subtext is a refusal. Completeness is a brochure.

Convenience confession. The declaration arrives when the scene is ready. Rain. A hospital corridor. A microphone. Distance, in the prior, is where truth is spoken. If your barrier wants the truth expensive, the model will spend it because the chapter heading said midpoint. A caused declaration is plot. An uncaused one is a hat.

Heat as substitute for attraction. Attraction, in Regis, is why this couple must stay in the room long enough to pay. Heat is what they do with their hands. The model will offer heat because heat is locally complete. Attraction that does not survive a Tuesday morning is not attraction. It is staging.

HEA without payment. The last chapter finds a ring, a job in the same city, a parent who was only ever kidding. Betrothal is supposed to be the visible commitment after recognition. Recognition is supposed to cost. The model treats the optimistic ending as a tone. Tone is not justice. Janice Radway, in *Reading the Romance*, argued that the readers she interviewed were not consuming formula so much as a reliable emotional justice and a heroine who came through. They could tell a bad book from a good one. They will tell yours.

Do not fix this by pasting "write like [beloved author]" into every prompt. The model will smuggle the beloved author's furniture and none of her costs. You have seen this leak. It is the encyclopedia smuggle wearing a corset.

## Document R

On the Soft SF week I asked you for Document I: want, method, cost, for one institution. Romance needs the same hiring document for a barrier. Call it Document R if you need a letter. Keep it short enough to check.

What the barrier wants. Not "they misunderstand." Misunderstanding is a mood. The job wants a transfer that would end the book. The family wants a name that cannot be spoken in that house. The secret wants to stay unpaid because telling it would make one of them unemployable, or unwelcome, or a different person than the one who entered the room. Want is specific. If you cannot point to the want on the page, the barrier is a preface.

How attraction keeps them in the room. Not chemistry. Chemistry is a mood with better lighting. She keeps taking the late shift because he is the only person who will say the true thing about the work. He keeps walking past the house because the window still faces the mill. Method is visible. If they only occupy the same space when the plot needs a kiss, you have not hired attraction. You have hired a schedule.

What HEA would cost. Who has to change a practice, not a feeling. Feeling-change is the model's favorite refund. He does not "realize he was wrong." He gives up the transfer, or the name, or the silence that was protecting him. She does not "open her heart." She stays in the city that costs, or she tells the secret to the person it will hurt. Cost is what the love scene will try to refund. Write the cost before you generate the love scene, or you will keep the gift and call it character development.

That is one sitting, model closed. It is Document I pointed at two people instead of a mill. Keep it next to the ledger, not inside the scene prompt. The scene gets two or three constraints the page actually touches. Then you run Document R against what arrives.

A two-hundred-page tropes list is a wiki. A wiki is a completeness machine. Completeness is what the model is for. Refusal is what you are for.

A miniature, because the method is easier to steal than the slogan. This is a hypothetical, not a lab story. Document R says: she cannot take the regional post without leaving the only person who has seen her work clearly; he cannot be in her mother's kitchen because the name he carries is the name that kitchen will not say; attraction is the late reports they still write together; HEA costs the post or the silence, and cannot cost nothing. Now you generate four hundred words that would like a rainstorm, a confession, and a private hour. What arrives, if you are not careful, is one of two kitchens from stock. Either the post vanishes because love and the kitchen is suddenly kind — barrier holiday. Or there is a new ex, a ball, and a gasp — fresh brochure. Strike both. Carry the floor: the post is still on the table; the name is still unspeakable *in that kitchen*; the late report is still the reason they are in the room. That is romance. The other was a heat chapter from the prior.

A caused change is still allowed. Someone says the name in the kitchen; we watch what the old mouth does. The post is taken, and we watch the attraction try to survive distance without a transporter. The barrier is allowed to lose in a scene. It is not allowed to have been kidding, and it is not allowed to be replaced by a complete new conflict the prompt invented overnight.

## Consent Is Physics

I am not writing a primer on on-page sex. I am writing about a constraint the model treats as mood.

The prior for heat is momentum. Momentum is not agreement. If your scene needs a spoken or enacted yes, and the model supplies a gasp, a trembling, a "she couldn't help herself," that is a refund of physics, not steam. Soft SF refunds a caste for tenderness. Romance refunds a will for heat. The reader who came for emotional justice will feel the floor move. They may not name the problem as "worldbuilding." They will name it as the book going wrong.

This is not a content rating. Sweet romance and explicit romance both have a floor. The floor is that the people in the room remain people. A generated body that only exists to complete a paragraph is completeness ethnography pointed at skin. You did not hire a body. The prior did.

In ["Hallucination, Confabulation, and the Limits of Generation"](/blog/hallucination-confabulation-limits) I said the model will invent a plausible fact rather than admit a gap. Romance inconsistency is that habit pointed at a yes. The invention is locally fluent. It is globally a lie about a person you already decided had a will.

You may use the model as a checker once the scene exists. Paste the scene and Document R — not the tropes list — and ask where the barrier got easier than the document allowed, and where heat arrived as a substitute for attraction. Do not ask "is this romantic." Romantic, to the model, means locally warm. Warm is the brochure. Then you verify. Do not accept a proposed new ex, storm, or confession from the checker. The checker is a sniffer. It is not a matchmaker.

## The Reader Already Knows

Romance readers are not a soft audience. They are a fast audience. Radway's Smithton readers trusted a bookseller named Dot because her judgment correlated with theirs and because she had read more than they had. The contemporary version of that desk is a thread, a newsletter, a store with a staff pick.

EJ, writing at Monster Romance Reviews after a 2025 scrape of AI-related posts across major romance subreddits, reported overall sentiment at −1.289 on a scale from −2 to +2, with "checking before buying" as the most common behavioral signal. One highly upvoted comment named the craft problem without using craft language: suspecting AI, the commenter said, is a fundamental misunderstanding of what people love about romance. The anger is not that a tool exists. It is that the contract was treated as furniture.

That is why purple polish is now a tell, and why hollow beats under too-perfect formatting showed up in that same writeup as something readers had learned to spot. In ["The Integrity Framework — A Writer's Code for AI Use"](/blog/the-integrity-framework-a-writers-code-for-ai-use) I said the human pass is where decisions live. Romance is a decision against the model's fluency in heat. Fluency in heat is the prior. A romance is a set of refusals that can stand being carried to an ending that still feels like justice.

If you skip Document R and keep only a tropes list, you can still write a couple. You will have a harder time writing a book. Two people who will not refund a yes can still grow a rainstorm behind the kitchen. Romance is the yes. The rain is only weather.

## For Next Time

On Wednesday we take the same problem into a genre that names its physics as fear: ["Genre: Horror"](/blog/genre-horror). Horror is not atmosphere plus a body. Its floor is a threat that does not refund when the lights come on. You have been practicing this on wells, titles, and barriers. Horror's model-prior is even thicker with convenience. The monster is explained. The house was never haunted. The last page takes the cost back. We will talk about hiring a threat the way you hired a barrier, and about catching the refund before it becomes a twist.

Your homework until then: write Document R, model closed — want, method, cost, for one barrier that has already touched the page, or that you are willing to hire before you generate. Then generate four hundred words that would like a rainstorm, a confession, and a private hour. Run Document R. Strike every purple prior, every barrier holiday, every declaration that arrived because the scene was ready. If the want is still audible, and more expensive because attraction had to keep them in the room without becoming a catalog of skin, the barrier is working. Bring it on Wednesday. Horror is wasted on a couple that will not keep their own word, and it is merciless on a couple whose heat was a brochure with a pulse.
