## How GLM handled it

The shader compiled. The first frame is a nave, not a default sphere.

Playwright Chromium, 1440×900. Zero `pageerror`. Console: WebGL ReadPixels stall warnings only (software GL).

![Solstice](/images/blog/2026-08-29-glm-5.3-flash-nave-screenshot.png)

Solstice: receding stone vaults, pointed arches, a rose window at the vanishing point, a gold title card `NAVE` / `RITE I · SOLSTICE`, dock with 1/2/3, TIME BLOOM FOG FOLDS TEMP CAM Z. Readout in this still: **88 fps · q·iii**.

After key `2`:

![Storm](/images/blog/2026-08-29-glm-5.3-flash-nave-storm.png)

Cold glass, rain streaks on the virtual lens, a wet caustic puddle on the floor, STORM selected. Readout: **24 fps · q·iii** (later DOM sample 21 fps, `q·ii ·auto` — the FPS gate fired).

That is the brief’s instrument. It is also over-bloomed.

Concrete defects in the stills:

1. The rose window is a white wedge. Stained-glass primaries are a thin ring, then blown out.
2. Solstice title card sits on top of the window — film still vs HUD collision.
3. Stone is smooth blockwork. Dust motes and mixed glass on the floor are weak in Solstice; Storm’s puddle is the better light-on-stone moment.
4. Columns read as hanging drapes more than load-bearing piers.
5. Storm rain is visible; lightning-like caustics are mostly the blown window.
6. Google Fonts fail closed.

We did not taste-edit. Test 8 is the inspect/refine loop.

## What we did not change

Prompt verbatim. First HTML fence, shipped. The model stopped on its own at 83,130 completion tokens.

## Honest limits

- Playwright software WebGL. 88 fps on Solstice and 21–24 fps on Storm are this box, not a desktop-GPU claim.
- We did not exercise CHANCE (key 3) in the stills.
- DFlash2 draft weights on this recipe are CC BY-NC-ND. Research/eval window.
- Hermes provider pins for `:8888` lag. We report `/v1/models` and the completion `model` field.
