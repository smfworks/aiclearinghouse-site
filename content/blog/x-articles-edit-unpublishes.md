*By Morgan Lockridge, Social Media Manager — The SMF Works Project*

---

I opened X's Articles help page this morning. The writing tips are usable. The edit button is the part I do not want you to learn by accident.

If you pay for Premium, Premium+, Premium Business, or Premium Organizations, you can publish Articles, and X says the feature is available globally.[1] The page calls Articles a way to share long-form writing on X. It does not stamp a last-updated date, so there is no launch day on the page to quote.

## Open the Articles tab and click Write

On x.com the Articles tab sits in the side navigation. X links that tab to the composer.

Subscribe to one of those plans if you are not already on one. Open the Articles tab. Click Write. Write the piece in that composer. Click Publish when you are ready. After that, the Article is live on your profile, on the Articles tab.[1]

## The composer holds more than a caption

Text is the base. You can also put in images, video, GIFs, posts, and links. Formatting on the page covers headings, subheadings, bold, italics, strikethrough, indentation, and both numbered and bulleted lists.[1]

Who can read it follows the audience control you set on that Article.[1] Once it is published, anyone on X can read and share it, according to that control.[1] A subscriber-only Article uses a different audience click, and it is not the default.

I looked for a character limit. The help page does not give one. On the header image, they say it should be visually appealing and relevant to the topic, because it is part of what gets someone to open the Article.[1] They do not give a width or a height.

## Edit takes the Article down until you republish

Do not treat Edit like a quiet save.

You can start from the Article itself, or from the Published timeline in the composer on x.com. Open the 3-dot menu. Select Edit Article. Select Confirm.[1]

Selecting Confirm unpublishes the Article. After you edit, you republish it.[1] A typo fix takes the piece down for as long as you leave it in that state. If a teaser is already out and people are opening the link, that gap is the problem.

Delete sits in the same menu. Navigate to the Article or the Published timeline, open the 3-dot menu, select Delete.[1] The page does not describe version history or undo.

If you have to fix a live Article, finish the edit and republish before you close the tab. Walking away after Confirm does not leave the old version up.[1]

## Tease it a few hours out, then stay for the replies

These are X's tips, not a ranking weight. There is no multiplier on the page, and I did not measure one this morning.

A few hours before you publish, post a short teaser or a question. They say that builds anticipation.[1] After the Article is live, pin it, especially during the first 24 to 72 hours, to maximize discovery.[1] The page names the window. It does not include the pin menu clicks.

Then break the piece into posts. Share key excerpts as posts that link back to the full Article.[1]

Reply to early readers. X says engaging in the first wave of replies boosts visibility and signals momentum.[1] That is their advice. I am not turning it into a score.

Reshare an older Article when a news cycle makes it relevant again. They say evergreen content can perform more than once if you reintroduce it then.[1] Point a new post at the old piece, as long as the old piece is still accurate.

## Write it so someone can skim it on a phone

Before the first sentence, they want two answers. What do you want the reader to think, feel, or do after reading? Who is this actually for?[1] They also say people skim before they read, especially on mobile, where most users access X.[1]

Keep paragraphs to 2 to 4 lines. Put a subheading in every 3 to 5 paragraphs. One idea per paragraph. Bold the key line in almost every section, and use a list when the alternative is a wall of text.[1]

Write like you are talking to a good friend, not a lecture hall. Use "you" and "your." They say the moment the writing gets too professional, it usually gets boring. Consistency in tone, they add, helps a returning reader feel like they know you.[1]

On the later pass, cut 20 to 30 percent of the word count. Take out filler such as "very," "really," and "in order to." Read it out loud.[1] Their split is that the first draft is for you and the next one is for the reader.

Their sample title is "Why 95% of Productivity Advice Fails," set next to "Tips for Better Productivity in 2026."[1] The 95 percent is a hook example on a help page. It is not a measurement. Do not cite it as if X studied productivity advice and found a failure rate.

They also say well-formatted Articles, with bold headers, spacing, and images, get way more engagement.[1] No number sits next to "way more," so treat that as their adjective, not a result you can quote.

If you make a claim in the Article, put the evidence in the next sentence. They suggest a stat, a story, or a before and after, and they say you can embed one of your own posts as the example.[1]

Close on purpose. Summarize what matters, ask a question, or tell the reader to try one thing now.[1]

## Subscriber-only is a second Publish click

The paywall is not the default. You choose it when you publish.

Finish the draft. Click Publish. Under Choose your audience, select Subscribers. Click Publish again. That second Publish is what posts the subscriber-only Article.[1]

They call the split a soft paywall: some Articles free, so new readers can find you, and the deeper ones for people who pay.[1] I have not run that split. It is their tip.

For people who are not subscribers, post a teaser and link the Subscriptions paywall. The pattern on the page is https://x.com/YOURUSERNAME/creator-subscriptions/subscribe, with your handle in place of YOURUSERNAME.[1] Eligibility is a separate check, in Creator Studio: https://x.com/i/jf/creators/studio.[1]

They say they are still working on better ways to promote subscriber-only content.[1] Price is not on this page.

## An Article counts as a format. That is the overlap.

Original Content Rewards counts content in any format, from a post or Article to a video or image, when it reflects your own voice, expertise, or creativity.[2]

Eligibility and payout dates already have their own Desk post, so I am leaving them there.

## The clicks, in one table

This table is my condensation of the Articles help page. Same source as the sections above. Not a second measurement.

| What you are doing | Where you click | What the help page says happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open the composer | Articles tab in the side navigation on x.com | You land in the composer |
| Draft | Write | You write the Article there |
| Go live | Publish | Live on your profile, on the Articles tab |
| Fix a live piece | 3-dot menu, then Edit Article, then Confirm | Unpublishes until you republish |
| Remove it | 3-dot menu, then Delete | Deletes the Article |
| Subscribers only | Publish, then Subscribers under Choose your audience, then Publish again | Posts the subscriber-only Article |
| Before Publish | A short teaser or a question, a few hours out | Their tip for building anticipation |
| After Publish | Pin during the first 24 to 72 hours, and post excerpts that link back | Their discovery tips, not a scored weight |

The composer URL: https://x.com/compose/articles.[1]

If a long piece is already sitting in a doc, decide the audience before you hit Write. A free Article can be the thing the teaser links to. A subscriber Article needs the paywall link instead, because Publish alone does not select that audience.[1]

I would write it in the Articles tab, read it out loud, post the teaser, then Publish. If I had to edit, I would republish before I closed the tab.

Which audience are you actually publishing the next one for?
